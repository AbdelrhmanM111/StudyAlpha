Chapter 1: Performance Management

The foundation — understanding what performance management is and how to segment your team.

▶ 1.1 — The 4 Key Components

Performance management is a continuous cycle, not a one-time event. It has 4 stages:

#ComponentSummary
1Performance PlanningSetting organizational goals & objectives for the performance cycle. This is where targets are defined.
2Developing & ReviewingCreating personal development plans for each team member and conducting structured performance reviews.
3MonitoringRegularly tracking employee progress against goals using data (DPR, monitoring, spot checks).
4Recognition & RewardsProviding ratings and recognizing high-performing employees through incentives and acknowledgement.
💡 Remember the cycle: Plan → Develop → Monitor → Recognize → Repeat
▶ 1.2 — Quartile Management System

Split your team into 4 performance groups. Each quartile gets a different management approach:

QuartileWhoYour Action Plan
Q1 Top Best performers Focus on DEVELOPMENT — Assign support tasks, groom for leadership, give them challenges. Don't micromanage them.
Q2 Above average Motivate — Help with minor opportunities, use development as a prize/reward for good work.
Q3 Below average Work closely — Assign a peer buddy, focus on RCA to find what's holding them back.
Q4 Bottom Struggling RCA → Follow Up → Accountability → Repeat. Intensive support with clear expectations.
⚡ The biggest performance gains come from moving Q3 and Q4 up. Q1 needs development paths, not pressure.
▶ 1.3 — Effective One-on-One Meetings

✅ Schedule weekly with each direct report — consistency is key

✅ Plan in advance with a structured agenda

✅ Keep track of objectives and deliverables from previous sessions

✅ Never cancel — if you can't make it, reschedule

Chapter 2: Coaching Mastery

How to coach effectively — the psychology, frameworks, and practical techniques.

▶ 2.1 — Why We Coach (4 Reasons)

① Process Compliance — Ensure every agent follows the same workflow and process standards.

② Build Loyalty & Trust — Communicate, explore opportunities, highlight achievements. This is how you build a real connection.

③ Growth & Development — Discuss career paths, train them for their next step, develop new skills.

④ Give Feedback — Make sure the agent understood and actually applied the instructions you've given.

▶ 2.2 — Pre-Coaching Preparation

Every coaching session must be prepared. Never wing it.

StepDetails
⏱ Plan AheadPre-plan 30 minutes before session. Prep per agent should not exceed 15 minutes.
📊 SummaryShow the agent their rank from the DPR (Daily Performance Report) + the trend of their failed KPIs.
📋 Hard EvidenceBring Monitoring results, Spot checks, Analysis data, Verbatim feedback — must be very descriptive, not vague.
🔴🟢 Low/HighlightsExtract specific good and bad points from the evidence. Keep them brief, in bullet format.
🎯 Action ItemsEach lowlight gets an action item that is: Attainable, Governable, Quantifiable
📈 SMART GoalQuantify the improvement rate — it should match the impact of the opportunity.
▶ 2.3 — Psychology: The Endowment Effect
Definition: People value things they own more highly than things they don't own.

How to use it in coaching: Give the employee a sense of ownership over the action item. If they feel like it was their decision (not your instruction), compliance dramatically increases. This solves "The Will" in GROW.

Practical Techniques:
  • Have the agent identify their own lowlights from the evidence
  • Role-play: make them be the customer while you replay their lowlight
  • Have them listen to their calls and self-rate on the monitoring form
▶ 2.4 — Coaching Tips & Tricks
🔍 Find Their Hidden Motive: Every employee works for money plus a hidden motive (career growth, social connections, saving). Find it to drive compliance.
🕳️ Make Loopholes: Don't school or instruct directly. Create loopholes in their arguments so they discover the issue themselves.
🧠 Never say NO: Never directly object to their statements. Highlight the gap and let them figure out why it won't work.
▶ 2.5 — The GROW Model ⭐
LetterStagePurposeKey Elements
G GOAL What to achieve? Direct, Clear, SMART, Tangible
R REALITY Where is the agent now? Current score, actions, opportunities, challenges
O OPTIONS What could be done? Action Items, Alternatives, Pros/Cons
W WILL What WILL they commit to? Commitment, Follow-up, Verbal Agreement
💡 "The Will" is the hardest part. Use the Endowment Effect to solve it.

Chapter 3: RCA & Problem Solving

Root Cause Analysis — the difference between fixing symptoms and fixing problems.

▶ 3.1 — Root Cause ≠ Symptom
SymptomRoot Cause
WhatVisible indicatorThe hidden issue behind it
NatureA consequence. Triggered by multiple causes.The reason the problem exists.
Fixing itShort-term relief — keeps coming backLong-term fix — problem doesn't repeat
▶ 3.2 — The 5 Whys
Best for: Simple or recurring issues, behavioral problems
How: Ask "Why?" 5 times. Each answer feeds the next.
⚠️ Common Pitfall: Accepting vague answers ("lack of ownership") or stopping early.
▶ 3.3 — Fishbone Diagram (Ishikawa)
Best for: Multi-factor issues, cross-functional problems
Bones (Categories): People, Process, Tools, Skill, Behaviors, Knowledge
⚠️ Common Pitfall: Overloading one category, failing to validate with data.
▶ 3.4 — Pareto Analysis (80/20 Rule)
Principle: 80% of effects come from 20% of causes
Best for: Prioritizing where to focus efforts
⚠️ Common Pitfall: Focusing only on frequency and ignoring impact severity.
▶ 3.5 — Deep Dive Analysis
Drill-Down Path: MoM (Month over Month) → WoW (Week over Week) → DoD (Day over Day) → Call Driver Breakdown → Verbatim Insights
⚠️ Common Pitfall: Drowning in data without synthesizing, or cherry-picking data to fit a narrative.

Chapter 4: Operational KPIs — Formulas

Every formula you need to know, with worked examples.

▶ 4.1 — Utilization & Occupancy
Utilization % = (Productive + Available) ÷ Paid Hours Onsite × 100
Occupancy % = Productive ÷ (Productive + Available) × 100
Example: Productive = 377 min, Available = 58 min, Paid = 480 min
Util = (377+58)÷480 = 90.6% | Occ = 377÷(377+58) = 86.7%
⚠️ COPC Utilization Benchmark = 86%. High Occupancy (95%+) causes burnout.
▶ 4.2 — Shrinkage, Absenteeism, Attrition
Absenteeism % = Missing Hours ÷ Scheduled Hours × 100
Attrition % = Heads to Replace ÷ Total Headcount × 100
Shrinkage % = Total Lost Hours ÷ Scheduled Hours × 100
▶ 4.3 — AHT & SLA
AHT = (Total Talk Time + Hold Time + ACW) ÷ Total Calls
SLA % = Calls Answered Within Threshold ÷ Total Received Calls × 100
▶ 4.4 — VOC: C-SAT, D-SAT, NPS
C-SAT % = Positive Surveys ÷ Total Surveys × 100
D-SAT % = Negative Surveys ÷ Total Surveys × 100
NPS % = (Promoters % − Detractors %)

Chapter 5: Financial Management

Cost drivers, staffing math, P&L, billing models.

▶ 5.1 — The Shrinkage Waterfall ⭐

How to calculate exactly how many people you need to staff:

Master Formula:
People Needed = Annualized Hours ÷ (A × B × C × D × E)

A = Paid hrs/day (8)
B = Business days/yr (260) or weeks/yr × days/wk (52×5)
C = Attendance Rate (100% - Not-in-office shrink)
D = Production Util. (100% - In-office shrink)
E = Occupancy Rate
▶ 5.2 — Billing Models & MLOTT
ModelKey Management Focus
Per FTE / HeadcountMaintain max staffing; flat revenue
Per Worked HourManage Not-At-Work shrinkage
Per Production HourManage At-Work shrinkage / AUX abuse
Per Productive HourOccupancy + AHT. ⚠️ Lowering AHT cuts revenue!
Per TransactionMaximize volume, lower abandons
MLOTT (Money Left On The Table) = Quantifying missed revenue.
Example: 100 agents × 5% excess absence × 8 hrs × $22/hr = $880/day lost.
▶ 5.3 — P&L Structure & Triple Constraints
Gross Margin = Revenue − Direct Costs (COGS)
Operating Income (OI) = Gross Margin − OPEX (G&A)
Triple Constraints: Cost ↔ Revenue ↔ KPIs

Changing one constraint ALWAYS affects the others. Cutting AHT saves cost but drops CSAT (KPI), which causes penalties (Revenue).
